Northwest Washington, D.C.

Glover Park

A quiet grid of 1920s rowhouses tucked into a ridge above Rock Creek — named for the banker who gave the city its park system, and never quite letting anyone forget it.

How a butcher's farmland became Glover Park

Four names, four decades, one neighborhood.

Before there was a neighborhood, this ridge above Rock Creek was the northern edge of Georgetown's farmland — plots the city called Wilberforce and, less charitably, Knave's Disappointment. German immigrant families and free Black market gardeners worked the soil, growing produce for Georgetown's markets.

Henry Kengla, a wealthy master butcher, spent decades quietly buying up land around what's now Tunlaw Road. When he died in 1906 at ninety-one, his holdings formed the core of what the neighborhood would become — bought soon after by banker and philanthropist Charles Carroll Glover, the man credited with creating Rock Creek Park itself.

Residential development was fitful at first. Then, in 1926, developer Benjamin H. Gruver began marketing rowhouses here as an "English village" — gas heat, hardwood floors, and a name that stuck. Glover Park appeared in print for the first time that year, and the streets started filling in.

The gardens are long gone, but the 1920s and '30s rowhouses they made room for are still the backbone of the neighborhood — joined by garden apartments, a stretch of Wisconsin Avenue shopfronts, and a border with Rock Creek Park's woods that hasn't moved in a century.

Finding your way around

A small grid with a few landmarks to steer by.

Main street
Wisconsin Avenue NW, the commercial spine running north from Georgetown
Neighbors
Georgetown to the south, Cathedral Heights to the north, Burleith to the west
Namesake street
Tunlaw Road, running through the land Charles Glover once purchased from Henry Kengla
Landmark uphill
The Washington National Cathedral, a short walk north

Green space, close by

The neighborhood Charles Glover's own park system nearly surrounds.

Glover-Archbold Park

A wooded stream valley trail running along the neighborhood's western edge, part of the National Park Service's Rock Creek Park system — the namesake's own legacy, still doing its job.

Guy Mason Recreation Center

The neighborhood's community hub on Calvert Street: a rec center, gardens, and the lawn where the annual Glover Park Day festival sets up its stage.

Whitehaven Park

A quieter trail spur just north, connecting into the wider network of parkland that laces through Upper Northwest.

Neighborhood life

What fills the blocks between the trees.

On Wisconsin Avenue

A walkable strip of restaurants, bars, and neighborhood shops carries most of the daily traffic — closer in scale to a small town's main street than to downtown D.C.

Schools

Stoddert Elementary and Hardy Middle School anchor the neighborhood's public education, both within walking distance of most homes.

Housing

Rows of 1920s and '30s brick rowhouses set the tone, filled out with garden apartment buildings and a scattering of postwar condos.

Getting there

Glover Park sits just far enough from a Metro station that the bus does the heavy lifting — the D2, D6, and 30-series lines run along Wisconsin Avenue and Whitehaven Parkway, connecting to Foggy Bottom and Tenleytown-AU stations at either end.
