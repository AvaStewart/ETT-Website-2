Washington, D.C.  ·  between Georgetown and Cathedral Heights

A wooded valley runs through the middle of this neighborhood.

Glover Park sits on the hill above the Potomac, split down the middle by a mile and a half of unbroken forest. Above ground it's rowhouses, a main street, and a rec center. Below the canopy, it's still a stream valley.

About the neighborhood

Glover Park takes its name from Charles C. Glover, the banker who donated the land that became the park running through it. The neighborhood itself grew up in the early twentieth century as rowhouses and apartment buildings filled in the hillside east of the valley, close enough to Georgetown to walk and far enough to have its own main street.

Wisconsin Avenue carries the commercial strip — restaurants, a hardware store, a Whole Foods that replaced a Safeway everyone still calls the "Social Safeway." Immediately west and south, the ground drops into forest.

Glover-Archbold Park

The trail

A 1.5-mile dirt trail follows Foundry Branch from Van Ness Street down to the Potomac, connecting to the Whitehaven and C&O Canal trails. No pavement, no lights — it stays a real forest trail, not a park path.

Guy Mason Recreation Center

The neighborhood's rec center sits at the park's edge on Calvert Street: a community room, a playground, and a garden plot program that's been oversubscribed for years.

The canopy

Tulip poplar, beech, and oak hold the slopes. The park is one of the largest unbroken stream valley forests inside the District, and it's managed jointly by the National Park Service and the D.C. Department of Transportation.

Get involved

The Glover Park Citizens' Association meets monthly and is the main channel for zoning questions, street issues, and park upkeep. The Friends of Glover-Archbold Park organize trail clean-ups a few times a year — muddy work, worth doing.

If you're new here: start at Guy Mason on a Saturday morning, or just walk the trail once. Most of what you need to know about the neighborhood is down there.
