History

The eastern part of the neighborhood grew out of butchering and market-garden land on the north edge of Georgetown; the western part came from land bought by banker Charles C. Glover.

Glover, an influential late-19th and early-20th century banker and philanthropist, purchased land from the estate of butcher Henry Kengla. Residential development was fitful at first, but took off in earnest starting in 1926 — the year the name "Glover Park" first appeared in print.

The neighborhood's rowhouses and apartment buildings date largely from the 1920s and 1930s. Developer Benjamin H. Gruver marketed the new homes on spacious rooms, gas heat, and hardwood floors, pitching the area as a kind of English village within the city.

Glover himself is remembered well beyond this hillside: he's credited with helping create Rock Creek Park, the National Zoo, and Embassy Row through land donations that shaped much of Northwest Washington.

Green edges

Two branches of Rock Creek Park frame the neighborhood, plus a park at its center.

Outdoors
Western border

Glover-Archbold Park

A wooded ravine and extension of Rock Creek Park, named for Charles Glover and Anne Archbold, who each donated part of the land. It forms the neighborhood's entire western edge along 42nd Street.

Southern border

Whitehaven Park

Another Rock Creek Park branch, marking where Glover Park gives way to Burleith and Georgetown to the south.

In the neighborhood

Guy Mason Park

Sits between Wisconsin Avenue and the Naval Observatory grounds, just south of Calvert Street — a softball diamond, a children's playground, and an informal dog park.

Who's here

A young, family-friendly Ward 3 neighborhood with a low crime rate.

Community

Housing

Mostly porch-front rowhouses from the 1920s and '30s, alongside apartment buildings — a mix that keeps the neighborhood denser and more walkable than much of upper Northwest.

Schools

Benjamin Stoddert Elementary serves the neighborhood and is consistently rated among the District's stronger public elementary schools.

Notable neighbors

  • Several foreign embassies, including the large Russian embassy compound
  • Washington National Cathedral, just north across Fulton Street
  • The U.S. Naval Observatory and the Vice President's residence, just east
  • Georgetown University, a short walk south

Boundaries

Wisconsin Avenue from Whitehaven Parkway north to Fulton Street, extending west to 42nd Street at the edge of Glover-Archbold Park.

Getting here

Glover Park sits between two Metro corridors rather than directly on one.
